Pharmasight — Replacing Guesswork in Pharmacy Supply Chains with a Collaborative BI Platform

My Role UX/UI and Research
Duration2 weeks
ToolsFigma · FigJam · Maze

The Problem

Pharmacies across Ireland and the UK operate on fragmented, manually maintained stock records. Transaction data is logged meticulously but used exclusively for billing, never for forward planning. The result is chronic dual failure: overstocking of slow moving medicines that expire on shelves and stockouts of critical medications patients depend on daily.

The brief was to design a business intelligence platform that could transform existing transaction data into actionable forecasting insight, shared simultaneously across wholesale distributors, pharmacy supervisors and clinical operations managers.

The Strategic Pivot

"Through user research, I identified that optimising a single pharmacy shelf addressed only a fraction of the problem. The real opportunity was at distribution level — serving multiple pharmacies simultaneously and reducing waste at scale."

This reframe was the most significant design decision of the project. The original brief pointed toward a single pharmacy stock management tool. Three stakeholder interviews revealed that supply chain pressure originated upstream. Designing for one shelf meant designing for one symptom. Designing for the distribution layer meant addressing the systemic cause.

User Personas

Three distinct users interact with Pharmasight. Each has fundamentally different goals, constraints, and mental models — requiring separate interface layers within the same platform.

Persona 01 · Pharmacy
💊
Sarah O'Brien
Pharmacy Supervisor
Detroit Pharmacy Plus
12 years experience
What she's trying to do
  • Catch critical stockouts before they affect patients
  • Stop spending 2+ hours a day on manual reconciliation
  • See alerts that tell her what to do, not just what's wrong
Where things break down
  • Her current system shows what's already gone — never what's about to run out
  • Too many low-signal alerts mean the urgent ones get buried
  • Any spare moment during peak hours is a moment she doesn't have
Her words →

"I need to know we're about to run out of insulin before it becomes a crisis. Not after."

Persona 02 · Operations
📋
Dr. Mark Connelly
Clinical Operations Manager
Regional Health Network
8 years experience
What he's trying to do
  • Order medicines that match what the local community actually needs
  • Spot pharmacies in trouble before they call him in a panic
  • Produce board reports without manually pulling data from six places
Where things break down
  • No network visibility — he's calling each pharmacy individually to understand what's happening
  • When AI makes a suggestion, he can't justify it to anyone without understanding why
  • He's accountable to a board but the data lives in silos he can't connect
His words →

"My system tells me what happened last month. I need something that tells me what's going to happen next week."

James (wholesale coordinator) starts his morning the same way he always does — checking what needs attention.

Starting point
He logs in and sees the network at a glance

The problem surfaces

Understanding the reasoning
He opens View Details before he does anything
This is the moment that matters. Budget Overview: current spend $134K vs AI-recommended $139K — with a projected profit lift of $15,340 monthly. He switches to Profit Comparison. Every product, line by line: current price, AI price, current quantity, AI quantity, profit impact. He can see exactly why the system is suggesting what it's suggesting.
Decision point
Accept the recommendation, or adjust it first?
He accepts as-is

Clicks Schedule Delivery. AI quantities carry over automatically. No re-entry, no second-guessing.

He adjusts first

Edits a quantity. The confidence score and profit impact update in real time — he can see the trade-off of every change before committing.

Outcome & Reflection

5 research insights from 3 interviews directly shaped 5 design features, each validated through think-aloud testing. Operating at distribution level rather than single pharmacy level creates value across the entire network simultaneously.

The Pharmacy Supervisor identified critical stock issues using traffic light alerts without additional navigation matching her time constrained workflow. The Clinical Operations Manager engaged meaningfully with drill down analytics her current system couldn't provide. The Wholesale Coordinator confirmed AI confidence scoring gave her the language needed for partner conversations.

"The most important design decision is often about identifying the right problem instead designing the right solution to the problem you were given."
